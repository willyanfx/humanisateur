A friend runs operations at a 40-person logistics company in Hamilton. Last September she added Claude to her team's workflow for about $80 a month. Within six weeks, the customer-service backlog dropped from 3 days to under 4 hours.

That's the story.

Not a revolution — the word makes me wince every time I hear it from someone who hasn't shipped anything. A toolkit. Boring. Useful.

I've watched maybe a dozen mid-sized companies try this over the past year, and the pattern keeps repeating: the ones who treat AI like a magic intern get burned, the ones who treat it like a junior hire who needs supervision get decent results, and nobody I know is "transforming" anything — they're just shaving hours off ordinary work, which is honestly enough. The trade-offs are real, but they aren't the ones the news cycle obsesses over. Data governance matters, sure. But the bigger issue in practice is that your best people stop explaining their reasoning, because the model writes it down faster than they can, and three months later nobody remembers why a decision was made. I don't know how to price that risk yet.

Rule of thumb. Dull, repeatable task where you already know what good looks like? AI will save you time. Judgment under uncertainty? It won't, and it'll sound confident while being wrong. Plan for that.
